His list

Ten criteria, rear and service above cost

The first four are the heavier ones. Cost is on the list. It is not the list.

01

Rear-seat comfort

XEV is the strongest sourced family bench: recline, flat floor, 3-across. No rear vents, even at the top spec. Sealion has the longest wheelbase (2930 mm), significant recline, chauffeur-suitable legroom, a low seat, and coupe duck-in. Reviewers split on Sealion under-thigh and a short backrest. Model Y: 5-seat power-recline bench, WB 2890. Harrier EV: no recline, 2 headrests, weak thigh, traditional roof. Atto 3: no recline, sits low, tight headroom. Sit the 9e and the Sealion with the usual passenger before paying for elegance. EC40: coupe fail versus the 9e and the Sealion. Short headroom, poor underthigh, no recline. Official rear head 932 mm, leg 917 mm (pano, may be a global template), WB 2702. Sit the Harrier for 20 minutes if the tall roof is the temptation. Sit the EC40 rear 20 minutes with a passenger over 5 ft 8.

02

Design

Elegance lean: Sealion. Ocean Aesthetics / Ocean X. Autocar: coupe-SUV, 19-inch RWD, contrast black. CarDekho: hide the badges, feels European; paint can be slightly better. TopGear: fluid and futuristic, does not scream. Dynamic and Premium share the body. Premium adds genuine leather and Tahiti Blue; Dynamic is black interior only, not genuine leather. XEV is flash and presence, not quiet luxury. Atto 3 is a polarizing gym cabin, not elegant. Model Y is not the elegant one. Harrier EV is the traditional SUV roof in the set. EC40 is an understated 4.44 m coupe versus the Sealion at 4.83 m. Design versus the Sealion is taste, not a space win.
